Product blueprint · working name

First Principles Studio

Talk to an expert out loud and break any idea or subject down to what is certainly true. A silent Scribe turns the conversation into a diagram, research, a verdict and a document. All of it runs on your own PC.

Two rooms, one voice loop

Pick a room. The examples below are illustrative: one sample idea and one sample topic, used to show what each screen holds.

You and the expert question the idea until every statement lands in one of three piles. The Scribe sorts them live. Sample idea: a solar cold box for vegetable vendors.

Facts

Checkable today
  • Leafy vegetables spoil faster when warm.
  • A solar panel only produces power in daylight.
  • Cooling moves heat out. It always costs energy.

Assumptions

Must be tested
  • Vendors lose enough stock to pay for a box.
  • A cart roof holds enough panel to run it.
  • Vendors would rather own than rent.

Open questions

Nobody knows yet
  • How much cooling does one crate need on a hot day?
  • What do vendors do about spoilage now?
  • Who repairs it when it breaks?

The Scribe draws how the idea works as blocks and arrows. Any link that rests on an assumption is flagged, so the weak points show on the drawing.

  1. Sun
  2. Solar panelfits on a cart?
  3. Batterylasts till dusk?
  4. Cooling unit
  5. Insulated box
  6. Fresh stock at closing time

From here the Scribe can also draft a clickable demo screen for software ideas.

Three separate checks. Items marked net need Online mode for real names and numbers. Offline, the Scribe prepares the structure and the list of what to look up.

Market fit

Is there room?
  • Direct competitors: who sells the same thing net
  • Indirect competitors: what people do instead net
  • The market today: size, growth, what is shifting net
  • Blue ocean room: what to eliminate, reduce, raise and create
  • Cost to enter net
  • Viable or not, with the reason

Product fit

Can it be built sensibly?
  • Does it solve the problem the talk uncovered?
  • Resource appetite: people, money, time, compute
  • Complexity map: the hard parts, named up front
  • Known unknowns: what could surprise you
  • Step-by-step path to launch the ideation phase

Customer fit

Are these the right people?
  • Personas: who they are and what they need
  • Segments: which group to serve first
  • Channels: where they gather and how to reach them
  • Market sizing net

TAM is everyone who could ever buy. SAM is the part you can serve. SOM is the part you can win first.

When several ideas are on the table, the Scribe scores them with RICE, the method from Intercom's product team. Score = Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort. These are example numbers. Change them and watch the ranking move.

Reach is people per quarter. Impact runs from 0.25 (minimal) to 3 (massive). Effort is person-months. ICE, Kano or MoSCoW can replace RICE in settings.

The Scribe lays out the evidence line by line. You make the call.

Outcome

Pursue

All three fits hold on facts and the score clears your bar. Next step: the smallest test that could prove it wrong.

Outcome

Park

One fit still rests on an untested assumption. The report names the test that would settle it.

Outcome

Drop

A fit fails on a fact. The report records why, so the idea does not come back unexamined.

      Four limits to design around

      A local model stops learning on its training date.Anything recent needs Online. Offline, the expert must say it does not know.
      Models invent links.So no link ever comes from the model's memory. Each one comes from the local library or a search result.
      Video does not play offline.Video links are saved with the session and open when you connect.
      Depth depends on your hardware.16 GB of memory is a comfortable floor for a mid-size model. Smaller PCs run smaller, shallower models.

      Saving and picking up again

      • Every session is a folder of plain files. You can read them without the app.
      • Open a saved session and the app loads its summary and the last few turns, then searches the rest for anything your next question needs.
      • The expert continues from where you stopped, in the same voice.
      • Experts are small text files too, so you can edit, copy and share them.

      If this becomes a product you sell, check licences early. Kokoro is Apache-2.0. Piper's engine is GPL-3.0 and each voice has its own terms.

      Fig. 5

      Build order

      Each stage is usable on its own, so you learn from real use before building the next one. Stages 4 and 5 can swap.

      1. The voice loop

        Speak to one expert offline and hear the answer. The transcript saves to a folder.

      2. The expert shelf

        Pick, edit and create experts. Saved sessions reopen with their context.

      3. The Scribe

        Live sorting into facts, assumptions and questions. Block diagram. The final document.

      4. Three fits and the Online switch

        Sourced market, product and customer research. Scoring. The verdict page.

      5. The Learn room

        Knowledge graph, offline Wikipedia, checked links, graphs that join across sessions.
